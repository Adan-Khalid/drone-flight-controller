# Design decisions

Each decision with the date, the reason, and what would change it. Calculations are in `reports/design_calcs.md`.

| # | Date | Decision | Why | Revisit if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | 2026-11-15 | Build a flight controller that runs ArduPilot | A board that drones and robots actually use; reusable in later drone projects | |
| D2 | 2026-11-15 | Design in KiCad 10 first, then redraw in Altium Designer | KiCad files are open to anyone and checkable in CI; Altium is the tool many companies use | |
| D3 | 2026-11-15 | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | 2 MB flash keeps the full ArduPilot feature set; in stock; hand-inspectable package | stock disappears before ordering |
| D4 | 2026-11-15 | ICM-45686 + BMI088 on separate SPI buses; DPS368 barometer; external compass; microSD logs | Two makers' sensors for two EKF cores; all in stock with ArduPilot drivers | a sensor goes out of stock |
| D5 | 2026-11-15 | LT8641 5 V buck at 2 MHz with the component values of ADI's 2 MHz 5 V typical application (RT 18.2 kΩ, 2.2 µH, 47 µF, 1 MΩ / 191 kΩ, 4.7 pF, 10 nF soft-start) | Calculations confirm it across 6.0 to 26.7 V (on-time limit 3.92 MHz, current limit 5.73 A, no subharmonic risk); compared with TI's LMR36520 and TPS54360 it runs coolest at 2 A (0.26 to 0.31 W conduction loss against 0.72 to 0.81 W, or a 0.76 to 1.14 W catch diode), uses the smallest parts and has the most current margin; about $10 more per board (report section 6) | the bench shows a problem |
| D6 | 2026-11-15 | TLV62569 buck (not the AP2112K LDO) for the processor and SD card 3.3 V rail | The AP2112K would reach 155 °C at typical load and 224 °C worst case at 45 °C ambient, above its 150 °C limit; the buck's conduction loss is 10 to 27 mW | |
| D7 | 2026-11-15 | Separate TPS7A2033 low-noise LDO for the motion sensors and barometer, after an RC filter (D12) | Low current (6.6 mA, 11.5 mW); high rejection at low frequency; the filter handles MHz switching ripple | |
| D8 | 2026-11-15 | Battery sense divider 150 kΩ / 10 kΩ with 100 nF (ratio 16.0) | Keeps the ADC pin at 3.03 V even at the 48.4 V surge clamp (analog pins accept no positive injection); 9.38 kΩ source impedance under the 50 kΩ limit | |
| D9 | 2026-11-15 | Power routed on outer layers or pours | 2 A needs 0.78 mm on a 35 µm outer layer but 4.68 mm on a 15.2 µm inner layer (IPC-2221, 10 °C rise) | thicker inner copper is ordered |
| D10 | 2026-11-15 | STM32H743 at 480 MHz needs silicon revision V (revision Y runs to 400 MHz) | DS12110 notes the 480 MHz limit applies to revision V; ArduPilot runs on both | the delivered chips are revision Y |
| D11 | 2026-11-15 | No LTspice; circuits are simulated in KiCad's built-in simulator (ngspice) | Keeps simulation inside the design tool; the regulator is checked by datasheet calculations and the scope, the passive circuits (battery divider, sensor supply filter, motor-signal edges) in KiCad | |
| D12 | 2026-11-15 | 10 Ω + 10 µF RC filter in front of the sensor LDO | Corner 1592 Hz, so the 1.5 to 2 MHz switching ripple is cut by about 60 dB (ideal parts); the sensors draw only 6.6 mA, so the resistor drops just 66 mV | the measured ripple at the LDO input is too high |
| D13 | 2026-11-15 | 220 Ω series resistor on each motor output | A shorted ESC wire then draws 15 mA, inside the processor pin's 20 mA limit (minimum 165 Ω); the edge into an assumed 100 pF still rises in 48 ns, 2.9 % of a DShot600 bit | the scope shows slow edges with the real ESC |
| D14 | 2026-11-15 | Pin map taken from ArduPilot's MatekH743 and MatekH743-bdshot board files, changed for this board's sensors | Same chip and package, already built and flown with ArduPilot; ArduPilot's own processor (`chibios_hwdef.py`, pinned commit) confirms every peripheral gets DMA and the IMU buses and motor timers have their own streams (`reports/firmware/hwdef_check.md`) | CubeMX or the schematic shows a conflict |
| D15 | 2026-11-16 | Schematic and board are generated by Python scripts from one circuit description (`hardware/gen/circuit.py`) | Every part, value and connection is reviewable as text and in git diffs; KiCad's ERC and a pin-by-pin comparison with KiCad's own exported netlist (`hardware/gen/check_netlist.py`) run on every change | the design moves to hand editing in KiCad or Altium |
| D16 | 2026-11-16 | Reverse-battery protection: LM74700-Q1 ideal-diode controller driving a TI CSD18543Q3A N-MOSFET | 60 V rating covers the 48.4 V surge clamp; 8.1 mΩ at 10 V gate drive, so 2 A wastes about 32 mW (computed, I²R); 3.3 x 3.3 mm and KiCad's library has its footprint; LCSC C840100, $1.35, 847 in stock on 16 Nov 2026. The 4.0 mΩ Infineon BSZ040N06LS5 ($1.53) was the other candidate | stock runs out |
| D17 | 2026-11-16 | microSD socket: Hirose DM3AT-SF-PEJM5, push-push | KiCad's library has a footprint for it, so no hand-drawn land pattern; LCSC C114218, $1.40, 14,742 in stock, 13.85 x 15.95 mm. The SOFNG TF-015 (C113206, $0.13) is cheaper but needs a footprint drawn from its drawing | the board goes to a larger run where the $1.27 difference matters |
| D18 | 2026-11-16 | 8 MHz crystal (Yajingxin TAXM8M4RFDCET2T, C403948, 12 pF load) with 15 pF load capacitors | Two 15 pF in series give 7.5 pF; with an assumed 4.5 pF of board and pin capacitance the crystal sees its 12 pF. The stray value is an assumption, so the frequency is checked on the bench | the measured clock is off by more than the crystal's tolerance |
| D19 | 2026-11-16 | TPS2116 power mux: the 5 V regulator has priority, USB takes over below about 4.48 V | PR1 divider 100 kΩ / 28.7 kΩ against the 1.0 V reference (0.92 to 1.08 V, TI SLVSFG1A) switches at 4.48 V typical, 4.13 to 4.84 V (computed); the board runs from USB alone and moves to the battery without a reset (requirement R4) | the bench shows a reset on switchover |
| D20 | 2026-11-16 | LT8641 SYNC/MODE pin left open: pulse-skipping mode | The pin offers Burst Mode (GND), pulse-skipping (open) or spread spectrum (INTVCC). Pulse-skipping "offers full frequency operation down to low output loads" (datasheet, pin functions), so the ripple stays at 2 MHz where the sensor filter (D12) removes it; Burst Mode's saving in quiescent current does not matter on a flight battery | conducted noise needs spread spectrum |
| D21 | 2026-11-16 | Status LEDs red and white (LCSC C2286 and C2290) | Both are JLCPCB Basic parts, so no extended-part loading fee |  |
| D22 | 2026-11-16 | (Superseded by D31: buzzer removed) Buzzer on two pads, switched by a 2N7002 with a 1 kΩ gate resistor, 100 kΩ pull-down and 1N4148W flyback diode | The processor pin cannot drive a buzzer directly; the pull-down keeps it silent while the processor boots; the diode catches the coil's kick |  |
| D23 | 2026-11-16 | I2C pull-ups: 1.5 kΩ on the external GPS bus, 10 kΩ on the on-board barometer bus | 1.5 kΩ is the Pixhawk DS-009 value for the GPS and compass cable; 10 kΩ is the value in the DPS368 application circuit |  |
| D24 | 2026-11-16 | (Superseded by D31: CAN removed) 120 Ω CAN termination through an open solder jumper | ArduPilot's CAN bus needs 120 Ω at each end; the jumper is closed only when this board is at the end of the bus |  |
| D25 | 2026-11-17 | Receiver (SERIAL7) and TELEM2 (SERIAL2) on solder pads, not JST GH connectors | With seven connectors the part courtyards filled about 78 % of both sides of the 36 x 36 mm board, too little room left to route; receivers are normally soldered anyway. The pin plan already allowed pads for TELEM2 | the board grows |
| D26 | 2026-11-17 | Parts on both sides: processor, 5 V regulator, connectors and LEDs on top; IMUs, barometer, sensor LDO, microSD and wire pads underneath | The parts do not fit on one side of 36 x 36 mm, so JLCPCB's Standard (double-sided) assembly is used, as agreed; big parts are placed by hand and each small part by a script at the free spot nearest the pin it serves (`hardware/gen/build_board.py`) |  |
| D27 | 2026-11-17 | Design rules: 0.1 mm track and space, vias 0.45 mm with 0.2 mm holes; battery-side power on 0.3 mm tracks plus local pours; 5 V on 0.25 mm tracks plus a pour on inner layer 2 | Inside JLCPCB's limits read on its capabilities page (0.09 mm track and space, 0.1 mm pad to track, 0.15 mm via hole, 0.2 mm via hole to track and hole to hole, 0.2 mm copper to a routed edge), and the via size costs nothing extra (0.2 mm holes cost more only with vias under 0.45 mm). The first layout with 0.127 mm rules and 0.5 mm vias left about half the connections unrouted on 36 x 36 mm. Plated through-hole to track 0.28 mm and SMD pads 0.15 mm apart are checked by custom rules (`hardware/kicad/dronefc.kicad_dru`) | the board grows or goes to 6 layers |
| D28 | 2026-11-17 | Routing by Freerouting through KiCad's Specctra export, then checked by KiCad's DRC | Open-source autorouter with a command-line mode, so routing is a repeatable script step; KiCad's DRC with JLCPCB's limits is the judge | the routing needs hand clean-up for signal quality |
| D29 | 2026-11-17 | Q1 uses a copy of KiCad's NexFET footprint with the gate pad 0.03 mm shorter on its inner edge | KiCad's footprint leaves 0.14 mm between the gate pad and the source pads, under JLCPCB's 0.15 mm for SMD pads on different nets; trimming the inner edge gives 0.17 mm and leaves the outer edge where TI's land pattern puts it | the fab's limit changes |
| D30 | 2026-11-17 | Freerouting runs from its Java build with its fan-out pre-pass and automatic neck-down switched off | The command-line build of version 2.5.0 crashes after its first pass; the fan-out pre-pass puts an escape via 2.5 to 4.5 mm from every surface pin, which filled the board; neck-down made 0.075 mm tracks, under the 0.09 mm limit | a later Freerouting release fixes the command-line build |
| D31 | 2026-11-18 | Stay on 4 layers and drop the CAN port, motor outputs 5 to 8 and the buzzer driver | Lowest cost was the priority. The 4-layer layout would not route with all features (18 connections open, the autorouter cutting the ground plane); 6 layers would have fixed it for about $50 more per 5 bare boards (JLCPCB quote, 18 Nov 2026: $78.40 against $28.02). Removing three extras a quadcopter does not need frees 20 parts and 8 processor pins, cuts parts and feeder fees, and leaves room to route. Everything a quad needs stays: 4 motor outputs, both IMUs, barometer, microSD, GPS, telemetry, receiver, USB | DroneCAN devices or a hexacopter are needed: then 6 layers |
